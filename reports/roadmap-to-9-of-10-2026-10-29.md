# Shop AI: improvement plan to reach 9/10

**Date:** 29 October 2026  
**Current assessment:** 7.5/10 overall; 8.3/10 as a demo; 6.3/10 for live restaurant use.  
**Goal:** A credible 9/10 for one real restaurant, earned through verified menu data, observed usability, repeatable AI evaluation, and recovery drills. The number is a product assessment, not a certification.

## Baseline and the main constraint

The latest completed code check passed 1,305 Vitest tests, 23 built-app system tests, typecheck, and build. The saved live `gemma4:12b` evaluation from 20 October passed 124/126 questions across English, Thai, and Burmese; the two identified cases now have code fixes, but Ollama was offline during the 29 October review, so the current model behavior has not been rerun. The 28 October single-laptop load test reported 99/100 open requests within 15 seconds when warmed and otherwise idle. These are controlled tests, not diner or kitchen evidence. See [`eval/results/live-open_questions.md`](../eval/results/live-open_questions.md) and [`docs/PERFORMANCE.md`](../docs/PERFORMANCE.md).

The sample allergen lists are based on research, not the restaurant's recipes. The current UI distinguishes *unknown*, *estimated*, and *verified*, and staff tickets show estimated data. However, `fitsProfile()` in [`app/src/modules/ai/ai.ts`](../app/src/modules/ai/ai.ts) currently accepts a listed but estimated dish when it does not list the diner's allergen. That is the first code change to make before a real allergy-profile pilot. The restaurant must provide recipe and supplier evidence; code cannot infer it.

## Release gates for a 9/10 assessment

| Area | Proposed gate | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Allergen safety | No estimated or unknown dish is suggested as fitting an allergy profile. Every *verified* active dish has a current recipe and supplier-label check, and staff explicitly confirm allergy requirements before preparation and service. No answer promises safety. | Owner/chef sign-off records, API and UI tests, observed staff rehearsal. |
| Diner and staff UX | At least 90% of representative task attempts succeed without assistance; no missed allergy warning or wrong-table order in the study. All critical tasks work by keyboard and screen reader; no focused control is hidden by floating UI. | Recorded task results across all three languages and roles; WCAG 2.2 AA audit. These are pilot thresholds, not population estimates. |
| AI answer quality | All safety-rule cases pass; at least 95% of a held-out, human-judged open-question set is both correct and useful in each language. At least 90% of warmed open requests finish within 15 seconds under the agreed pilot load; failures show the staff path promptly. | Fresh repeated live evals, native-language review, latency distribution, model-off fallback test. A zero-error sample is not a safety guarantee. |
| Privacy and recovery | The owner approves a raw-chat retention period and deletion workflow; notices and README describe actual storage. A daily off-device backup includes the database, uploads, and required secrets; a restore drill meets the pilot's proposed 24-hour recovery point and two-hour recovery time. | Policy decision, deletion tests, dated backup and restore log, integrity check. |
| Operational pilot | One restaurant runs a controlled pilot for at least a week with no lost orders, no critical allergy or privacy incident, and a resolved or documented response to every severe issue. | Incident log, daily owner/chef review, final sign-off. |

**Score rule:** Do not call the product 9/10 merely because code changes pass CI. Safety, privacy, and real-service gates must pass. A failed critical gate caps live-readiness below 9 even if the interface looks excellent.

## Evidence behind the priorities

- The [UK Food Standards Agency's restaurant guidance](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-guidance/allergen-information-for-non-prepacked-foods-best-practice-voluntary-information) recommends current recipe/ingredient records, supplier checks, cross-contact information, and a staff conversation. This is a useful design benchmark; it is **not** asserted here as Thai restaurant law.
- [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) covers focus visibility and interaction-triggered motion; [W3C's user-evaluation guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/involving-users/) explains why conformance checks alone do not show real usability.
- [NIST's AI RMF Core](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/) recommends context-specific measurement and post-deployment monitoring. The suggested holdout and pilot are an application of that guidance, not a NIST-required sample size.
- [NIST's data-minimization definition](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/minimization) supports limiting stored personal data and keeping it only as long as needed. A retention period must be chosen for this restaurant's purposes and jurisdiction.
- [SQLite's online backup guidance](https://www.sqlite.org/backup.html) explains consistent snapshots of a live database; [SQLite's integrity-check documentation](https://www.sqlite.org/pragma.html#pragma_integrity_check) supports the restore verification step.

## First milestone to start now

1. Make `fitsProfile()` reject `estimated` and `unknown` allergen lists; add regression tests for AI recommendations and the diner flow.
2. Fix the README/privacy overclaim and add the restaurant/menu identity smoke check.
3. Prepare a one-page recipe-and-supplier verification sheet for the owner/chef. Until they sign it, keep all research-derived dishes marked estimated.

This milestone improves the highest-risk behavior without waiting for the visual research or the live model. The larger score increase depends on the restaurant's evidence and observed user behavior.
